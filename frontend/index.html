A year in Odisha

Follow the seasons, not a checklist.

Odisha's cultural calendar and its natural calendar overlap. This is a general orientation, not a wildlife guarantee: always check local conditions, closures and community rules before travelling.

Open the map
01

Winter

November – February

Wetlands fill with migratory birds. Chilika and other lagoons are the classic example; keep a respectful distance and follow local guiding rules.

Wetlands and birding
02

Spring

February – March

Flowering trees and temple festivals arrive together. Palash and other blossoms are visible along roads and forest edges; cultural places get busy.

Culture and blossoms
03

Summer

April – June

Mango season and hot afternoons. Gulmohar flowers along many roads; plan outdoor visits early and carry water. This is not a good season for long forest walks.

Parks and gardens
04

Monsoon

July – October

Greenery and waterfalls return, with travel disruption. Check roads and closures before setting out; some forest routes are seasonal.

Waterfalls and rain

Season labels are broad orientation, not a forecast. Festival dates follow the lunar calendar and change each year.

How trust is handled

What the AI can and cannot tell you

Confidence is not certainty

A softmax score is the model's preference among the classes it was trained on. It is not the probability that the identification is correct, and it is not a conservation record.

It abstains on weak evidence

Small, dark, blurry or out-of-distribution photos are rejected before classification. Close top-two scores and implausible locations trigger “needs review” instead of a confident answer.

Missing data stays missing

Tourism timings, fees, contacts and accessibility notes are only shown when a source recorded them. Otherwise the page says “Not verified” and invites a sourced correction.

Photograph something interesting?

Run a quality-checked identification. You can keep the result without saving it, or consent to store it as a pending observation.
